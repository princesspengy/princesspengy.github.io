Feebz: A Virtual Pet Game by Bee Braun and...Me!

    4 min read

    I just released a video game in collaboration with bee braun, a loved one of mine who told me that since I like video games so much I should really see about learning how to make one. we decided to scope out a game jam that we could participate in, eventually settling on the 87th godot wild jam, which ended up having the very interesting theme of "decay", and settled on a game where you explore a dead website, try to bring back your dead virtual pet, and learn the truth behind the promise that everything lives forever on the internet.

    check out the itch page if you wanna play it! and once you're done playing, you can keep reading for a dev diary type thing where I talk about the creative process a little bit!

    our first few ideas were, of course, rather literal ones. most of them had a basis of technological hardware—as someone with a decade-old laptop and a strong personal association with robots, it was the first place my mind went! but then we hit on the fact that websites also undergo a sort of decay as image links break and tools like adobe flash are shuttered.

    and then bee had the idea everything else spun out from: what if you came back to an abandoned virtual pet website and found that your pet had died?

    I've talked about this in my diary entry on remember places? and dark forest virtual chatroom, but virtual spaces and digital preservation are really important to me as a lifelong internet resident. this game let bee and I tell a story similar to those, but with more of a focus on nostalgia and broken promises of permanence.

    I realized fairly early on that this game is very much within the archetype of "ruined childhood media", what with being a haunted trek through an innocent nostalgic website. but during our talks about what to do with the more unnatural elements of the game, I made one thing very clear to bee: the website is not evil. the website does not have a spooky demon in it.

    because more interesting than just being evil, I saw it as a chance to explore that dread and disorientation of experiencing a part of such a magical place as the internet being torn away. navigating the website was envisioned as a sobering trip through a graveyard in remembrance of the fact that nothing actually lives forever on the internet.

    seeing a broken image link is a little like seeing roadkill, for me. here is a thing that once was and will never be again. forgotten collateral damage of a frantic and senseless world. there's a discomfort to it that felt more personally resonant than any corruption or other hints of subversion, and so a priority was to make liberal use of it and other similar signifiers (like bee's genius idea to make non-functional minigames) as a tone-setter. it also, conveniently, made for a very good excuse to not have to make like half of the visual media such a website would have had

    as for the gameplay, we wanted to capture the feeling of poking around a website and finding arg-like secret pages, partially as something that evoked internet nostalgia in us but also just something we both thought was neat as a framing device. bee was the one to suggest an actual goal this could lead up to: a doomed attempt to bring back your pet.

    fun fact: I originally wanted the process to just result in returning to the pet page only for apollo to be reaplced with another broken image, but bee convinced me that being locked back out of the website and leaving the player in inconclusive doubt as to how real any of that was supposed to have been would hit the player much harder

    the forum threads were my idea, and they almost didn't make it in. I wanted very much to be able to write things for the game that brought out those themes and acted as a little collection of short stories. but templating the forum threads turned out to be easier than expected, and so we got to put them in! bee wrote the thread about the buyout by redroot, and I wrote the other two

    I pitched the forum threads to bee as something that occupied a dubious and liminal space in the narrative of the game. bee's thread stuck to drawing dread and discomfort from the dramatic tension of seeing a bunch of children learning about a corporate process we've seen happen many times before, where my threads incorporated more unreal elements that wouldn't be seen in a genuine forum thread of the time.

    the out-of-time messages of the feebz sharing thread are obviously erroneous, and arcanyx obviously wasn't using an empty forum thread as a diary over a longer course of time than those forums even existing. it was more about settling the reader into an emotional space that resonated with the themes we wanted to hit on, like the intrinsic melancholy of a bunch of kids enthusiastically sharing and talking about something you can't see and will never be able to see

    meanwhile, the arcanyx thread was something I entirely wrote in a single late-night fit and is where I decided to let loose a little bit regarding a topic that I very much associate with the theme of decay and online spaces: watching relationships as they fall apart and not knowing why.

    feebz was a delight to make, even if most of what it got me to learn about godot was how ui buttons work and how to put blocks of text into varyingly complicated assortments of boxes. it's very refreshing to have made art and shown it to people. I'm hoping to do it again
