Strange Gaming Diaries: catgirl bloodhunt, by quiet4miu

    3 min read

    this game was brought to my attention by a friend who makes a habit of foraging through the steam releases page by sorting by release date. she's a brave little creature that I think we should all aspire towards

    since the statistics would suggest that I'm one of like fifty people to have ever played this video game, I'll offer more of an overview of the game than I normally would. catgirl bloodhunt is a game based on a very clever combination of very simple elements:

    1. when you start the level a timer counts down that kills you if it reaches zero
    2. killing enemies increases this timer
    3. you also die if you shoot a certain number of times without getting an ammo pickup
    4. your movement is like a pokemon sliding ice puzzle except you slide instantaneously

    what this results in is a hectic, white-knuckle shooter/puzzle hybrid that tests reaction time and mental flexibility. ammo pickups are constantly needed to not die, but spawn in different locations each time, necessitating constant improvisation of your movement paths to keep up a steady supply of bullets and bloodshed.

    it's really fun! the striking visual design ensures things never get too noisy to figure out, and the rapid pace with which you get back into the action after dying allows navigation through the level to slip into muscle memory. the mechanics of the game's many enemies are never explained, but are intuitive enough that I never felt confused to the point of frustration and got to have a few moments of "OHHHHH" during the experience

    and there's something very, very deeply charming about the game's sense of artistic style. the presentation hit some familiar notes for me, definitely feeling in conversation with the culture of girlhurting cultivated by the likes of [that's vn club](https://thatsvn.club/) and their contemporaries. the grungy environmental aesthetic combined with the cutesy low-detail sprite work gives it a particular ultraviolent gap moe that I find delightful

    the doubtlessly frantic mindset cultivated by the game is also compellingly mirrored by our player character, the eponymous catgirl whose name is also named catgirl. she trembles with her weapon and squints her eyes shut with every pull of the trigger, her helplessness never feeling at odds with her body count. her wordless journey through these carnage-soaked industrial mazes is compelling, and the unlockable portraits show that most of the other creatures in the game are just as disarmingly adorable as she is.

    there's even quite a range of emotional tones hit by the game's levels too. the double-sunny level is menacing and seething, the penultimate level is crowded and messy, and the introduction of the ghosts is almost beautifully eerie. this is helped by the music, a playfully eclectic assortment of tracks featuring bossa nova, bagpipes, breakbeats, and hatsune miku.

    all in all this game fucking rocks. there's a lot of room for skill expression here, and it hit me with fond memories of these exact kinds of little tight-knit trinkets back in the days of newgrounds flash games. it has a more primordial sort of feeling than a lot of other small-scale productions I've seen, a rawness that emerges from having made something that just feels really fucking fun the moment you boot it up. it's awesome and I want everyone reading to play it and say nice things about it
